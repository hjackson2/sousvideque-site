Doneness chart (about 1½ in thick)

This is a doneness preference chart from Anova and Serious Eats, not a USDA consumer minimum. Timing is given for pork chops about one-and-a-half inches thick. For thicker, double-cut chops, both say to add 15 minutes to the minimum time for each extra half inch. Anova and Serious Eats both limit pork-chop cook times to under 4 hours (at four hours the meat has begun to lose some of its resilience). Their favorite temperature is 140°F / 60°C (medium-rare: tender, juicy, and meaty).

Thickness and pasteurization (Baldwin)

Douglas Baldwin separates heat-through time (core approaching bath temp) from pasteurization time. Example from his Table 2.2 (thawed, slab-like): a 25 mm (~1 in) piece needs about 1¼ hours to heat through; a 50 mm (~2 in) piece about 3½ hours. He warns: do not use heat-through tables alone to claim pasteurization—use his meat pasteurization Table 5.1 (beef, pork, and lamb) when you need that reduction. For traditional-style pork chops he cooks thawed chops in a 141°F / 61°C bath for the times in Table 5.1. His separate “Slow Cooked Pork Chops” recipe is a 131°F / 55°C bath for 12 hours—a long tenderizing cook, not the 1-to-4-hour preference chart above. A few rows from Table 5.1 (starting from 41°F / 5°C; at least a million-to-one reduction in Listeria, a three-million-to-one reduction in Salmonella, and a hundred-thousand-to-one reduction in E. coli):

Baldwin writes that pork can be safely cooked at 130°F / 54.4°C, and that raw or unpasteurized food must never be served to highly susceptible or immune-compromised people.

USDA FSIS consumer minima (traditional cook)

USDA FSIS consumer guidance for pork steaks, chops, and roasts is a minimum internal temperature of 145°F / 62.8°C, then rest at least 3 minutes, measured with a food thermometer. Ground pork is different: 160°F / 71.1°C. Leftovers: reheat to 165°F / 73.9°C. That is the agency’s traditional-cook consumer chart—not a sous vide time-at-temp schedule. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours (1 hour if the air is above 90°F); keep the fridge at 40°F or below; leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge or 3 to 4 months in the freezer (quality). The danger zone FSIS teaches for leftovers is 40°F to 140°F; keep hot food at 140°F or warmer.

If you cook below the USDA consumer pork-chop minimum for preference (as the 130°F and 140°F rows on the Anova/Serious Eats chart do), understand the risk, serve promptly, and do not serve unpasteurized food to people who are immunocompromised. Anova and Serious Eats both say that so long as you are cooking above 130°F / 54°C, they associate no real health risks with a prolonged hold — and Anova warns: never chill and reheat food that was cooked or held below 130°F / 54.4°C. When in doubt, cook to USDA 145°F + 3 minutes rest, or use a Baldwin Table 5.1 pasteurization hold.

Sources: FSIS Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart (last updated Apr 14, 2025); FSIS Leftovers and Food Safety (last updated Jul 31, 2020); Anova pork chop guide; Serious Eats; Baldwin.

Quick workflow

  1. Preheat the bath to the temperature for the doneness you want from the Anova/Serious Eats chart, and let it reach temperature before adding the pork (Serious Eats).
  2. Season, bag (vacuum or water-displacement), and fully submerge. Cook within the timing range for about 1½-inch chops — and add 15 minutes to the minimum for each extra half inch on thicker cuts. Do not go past 4 hours (Anova, Serious Eats). From frozen, both say to add an extra hour so the pork can thaw.
  3. Remove, pat very dry, then sear hard and fast in a smoking-hot skillet until the crust is deep brown and very crisp, about 45 seconds per side, and brown the edges (Anova, Serious Eats). Anova’s recipe then rests the chops just a couple of minutes and pours the reheated pan drippings over them.
  4. Verify with an instant-read thermometer if you are aiming for the USDA consumer minimum or a Baldwin pasteurization hold.

Anova and Serious Eats both say brining is unnecessary for sous vide pork chops. If you will bag the chops and refrigerate them for a day or two before cooking, they season just before the sear, not in the bag, because salt can give pork a cured, ham-like texture.
