Texture chart (preference guide)

This is a doneness preference chart from Anova and Serious Eats, not a USDA consumer minimum. Both pick the bath temperature for the texture you want, then hold within the timing range. Serious Eats and Anova both recommend never cooking chicken breast longer than 4 hours (Serious Eats adds that two is even better), because long holds turn it mushy. Their preferred range for chicken served hot is 140°F / 60°C to 145°F / 63°C.

TextureBath temperatureTiming range
Very soft and juicy, served hot140°F / 60°C1½ to 4 hours
Juicy, tender and slightly stringy, served hot — or tender and juicy served cold (chicken salad)150°F / 66°C1 to 4 hours
Traditional, juicy, firm and slightly stringy, served hot160°F / 71°C1 to 4 hours

Sources for the table above: Anova Culinary — Chicken Breast (temperature and timing chart) (J. Kenji López-Alt) and Serious Eats — The Food Lab’s Complete Guide to Sous Vide Chicken Breast. Serious Eats notes that around 155°F / 68°C chicken breast starts to turn chalky and tacky, and says to add an extra hour when cooking straight from frozen. Anova’s Ultimate Sous Vide Chicken Guide gives the same chart.

Thickness and pasteurization (Baldwin)

Douglas Baldwin writes that chicken and turkey breast cooked sous vide can be cooked to a medium doneness (140°F / 60°C to 150°F / 65°C) while still being pasteurized. His recipe: cook thawed breast in a 146°F / 63.5°C bath for the times in his Table 4.1. That table gives the time for at least a million-to-one reduction in Listeria and a ten-million-to-one reduction in Salmonella, starting from 41°F / 5°C. A few rows from it:

USDA FSIS consumer minimum (traditional cook)

USDA FSIS consumer guidance for all poultry (breasts, whole bird, legs, thighs, wings, ground poultry, giblets and stuffing) is a minimum internal temperature of 165°F / 73.9°C, measured with a food thermometer. Reheat leftovers to 165°F / 73.9°C. This is the agency’s traditional-cook consumer chart—not a sous vide time-at-temp schedule.

Source: FSIS Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart.

Why lower temps can work: FSIS time-at-temperature tables

FSIS’s cooking guideline for meat and poultry plants (Revised Appendix A, Table 3) lists how long chicken must be held at a given internal temperature to reach a 7-log reduction of Salmonella. The hotter the meat, the shorter the hold. It is written for inspected establishments, and FSIS lists relative humidity and come-up time as critical operating parameters. It is context, not a home-cook recipe. A few rows:

These hold times start after the center reaches that temperature, which Serious Eats says can take up to about 45 minutes; it recommends adding an extra hour to the government times for chicken from the fridge, or two hours from frozen. FSIS also recommends limiting the total time product temperature is between 50°F and 130°F to 6 hours or less. Sources: FSIS Cooking Guideline for Meat and Poultry Products (Revised Appendix A), Table 3; Serious Eats Food Lab.

Caution: Douglas Baldwin writes that raw or unpasteurized food must never be served to highly susceptible or immune-compromised people, and that only immune-competent people should eat unpasteurized food, knowing the risks. If you are cooking for someone who is immunocompromised or otherwise highly susceptible, or you are unsure your bath and thermometer are accurate, follow the USDA 165°F consumer minimum. Use a calibrated thermometer, and do not cut the hold times short.

Quick workflow

  1. Preheat the bath to the temperature for the texture you want from the chart above, and let it reach temperature before adding the chicken (Serious Eats).
  2. Season, bag (vacuum or water-displacement), and fully submerge. Cook within the timing range—long enough for the thickness (Baldwin) but not past 4 hours (Anova, Serious Eats).
  3. To serve hot: pat dry and sear skin-side down in a hot skillet until the skin is deep brown and crisp, about 2 minutes total (Serious Eats).
  4. For chicken salad: drop the bags into an ice bath until fully chilled, about 10 minutes (Serious Eats, Anova), then refrigerate.
