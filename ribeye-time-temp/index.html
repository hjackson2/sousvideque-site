Doneness chart (1½–2 in thick)

Anova Culinary and Serious Eats give these bath temperatures and timing ranges for steaks one-and-a-half to two inches thick. For steaks one inch thick or less, they say the initial cook can be shortened to 40 minutes. They also state: steaks cooked under 130°F / 54.4°C should not be cooked longer than 2½ hours at a time for food-safety reasons.

DonenessBath temperatureTiming range
Very rare to rare120°F / 49°C to 128°F / 53°C1 to 2½ hours
Medium-rare129°F / 54°C to 134°F / 57°C1 to 4 hours (2½ hours max if under 130°F / 54°C)
Medium135°F / 57°C to 144°F / 62°C1 to 4 hours
Medium-well145°F / 63°C to 155°F / 68°C1 to 3½ hours
Well done156°F / 69°C and up1 to 3 hours

Source for the table above: Anova Culinary — Sous Vide Ribeye Steak (J. Kenji López-Alt), matching the strip/ribeye chart in Serious Eats — The Food Lab Complete Guide to Sous Vide Steak. Anova notes highly marbled ribeye/strip often taste best around medium-rare to medium (129°F / 54°C to 135°F / 57°C).

Thickness vs heat-through (Baldwin)

Douglas Baldwin’s free guide separates heat-through time (core approaching bath temp) from pasteurization time. Example from his Table 2.2 (thawed, slab-like): a 25 mm (~1 in) steak needs about 1¼ hours to heat through; a 50 mm (~2 in) steak about 3½ hours. He warns: do not use heat-through tables alone to claim pasteurization—use his meat pasteurization Table 5.1 (or an equivalent validated table) when you need that reduction. For tender ribeye he lists preference anchors of rare 125°F / 51.5°C, medium-rare 130°F / 54.5°C, and medium 140°F / 60°C.

Source: Douglas Baldwin — A Practical Guide to Sous Vide Cooking (Tables 2.1, 2.2, 5.1).

USDA FSIS consumer minima (traditional cook)

USDA FSIS consumer guidance for intact beef steaks is a minimum internal temperature of 145°F, then rest at least 3 minutes, measured with a food thermometer. That is the agency’s traditional-cook consumer chart—not a sous vide time-at-temp schedule. Ground beef is different: 160°F. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours (1 hour if the air is above 90°F); keep the fridge at 40°F or below; reheat leftovers to 165°F. The danger zone FSIS teaches for leftovers is 40°F to 140°F.

If you cook below the USDA consumer steak minimum for preference (as many sous vide charts do), understand the risk, serve promptly, and do not serve unpasteurized food to people who are immunocompromised. Baldwin and Anova both flag long holds under ~130°F. When in doubt, cook hotter/longer or follow USDA 145°F + rest.

Sources: FSIS Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart; FSIS Leftovers and Food Safety; FSIS Keep Food Safe! Food Safety Basics.

Quick workflow

  1. Preheat the bath to your chosen doneness from the Anova/Serious Eats chart.
  2. Season, bag (vacuum or water-displacement), and cook for the timing range that matches thickness.
  3. Remove, pat very dry, then sear hard and fast in a smoking-hot pan or on a hot grill (Anova finishing steps). Sous vide steak does not need a traditional rest.
  4. Verify with an instant-read thermometer if you are aiming for a USDA consumer minimum or pasteurization hold.
