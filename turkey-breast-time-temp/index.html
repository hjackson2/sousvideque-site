Preference chart (breast)

This is a doneness preference chart from Serious Eats, not a USDA consumer minimum. Kenji sets the bath a couple of degrees above the target core so the breast comes up a little faster, then holds after the core is at temperature. His favorite is a 145°F / 63°C bath for about 2½ hours total — white, tender, and moist, close to traditional roast turkey in texture but moister. The recipe starts from a large whole skin-on, bone-in turkey breast (about 5 pounds), with the two halves tied into an even cylinder. Come-up on a large breast can take an hour or more; the hold clock starts only after the center reaches the core temperature in the chart.

The 132°F bath / 130°F core row is a texture preference from Serious Eats and Anova, not an FSIS Appendix A hold. Table 4 for turkey starts at 136°F / 57.8°C. Do not treat 130°F as a USDA-validated pasteurization temperature. If you cook that row, understand the risk, serve promptly, and do not serve unpasteurized food to people who are immunocompromised.

Thickness and pasteurization (Baldwin)

Douglas Baldwin writes that chicken and turkey breast cooked sous vide can be cooked to a medium doneness (140°F / 60°C to 150°F / 65°C) while still being pasteurized. His recipe for either breast: cook thawed meat in a 146°F / 63.5°C bath for the times in his Table 4.1. That table gives the time for at least a million-to-one reduction in Listeria and a ten-million-to-one reduction in Salmonella, starting from 41°F / 5°C — it already includes come-up, so you do not add a separate hold on top of these times. He warns: do not use heat-through tables alone to claim pasteurization. For reference, his Table 2.2 heat-through times (thawed, slab-like, core to within 1°F / 0.5°C of the bath) are about 1¼ hours at 25 mm (~1 in) and about 3½ hours at 50 mm (~2 in). USDA FSIS consumer minimum (traditional cook)

USDA FSIS consumer guidance for all poultry (breasts, whole bird, legs, thighs, wings, ground poultry, giblets and stuffing) is a minimum internal temperature of 165°F / 73.9°C, measured with a food thermometer. Reheat leftovers to 165°F / 73.9°C. This is the agency’s traditional-cook consumer chart—not a sous vide time-at-temp schedule. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours (1 hour if the air is above 90°F); keep the fridge at 40°F or below; leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge or 3 to 4 months in the freezer (quality). The danger zone FSIS teaches for leftovers is 40°F to 140°F; keep hot food at 140°F or warmer. Cut leftover turkey into smaller pieces to cool faster: slice the breast; legs and wings may stay whole. An ice or cold water bath before the fridge is an FSIS option for rapid chill.

Sources: FSIS Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart (last updated Apr 14, 2025); FSIS Leftovers and Food Safety (last updated Jul 31, 2020).

Why lower temps can work: FSIS time-at-temperature tables

FSIS’s cooking guideline for meat and poultry plants (Revised Appendix A, Table 4) lists how long turkey must be held at a given internal temperature to reach a 7-log reduction of Salmonella. The hotter the meat, the shorter the hold. These times start after the center reaches that temperature. FSIS lists relative humidity and come-up time as critical operating parameters for inspected establishments. The table is context, not a home-cook recipe, and FSIS gives no come-up time by thickness — probe the thickest part and start the hold when it reads the target. Serious Eats says come-up on a large turkey breast can take an hour or more, and recommends checking after the first hour and every 15 minutes, or using a leave-in probe. FSIS footnote 11: a 7-log reduction of Salmonella is achieved instantly at internal temperatures whose holding time is 0 seconds. Serious Eats prints the 1% fat column and writes “Under 10 seconds” at 165°F. Times at or above 145°F sit inside the table’s blue box (eligible for FSIS relative-humidity options 1 and 2); the 136°F and 140°F rows are outside that box (humidity options 3 and 4 only, footnote 12). Caution: Douglas Baldwin writes that raw or unpasteurized food must never be served to highly susceptible or immune-compromised people, and that only immune-competent people should eat unpasteurized food, knowing the risks. If you are cooking for someone who is immunocompromised or otherwise highly susceptible, or you are unsure your bath and thermometer are accurate, follow the USDA 165°F consumer minimum. Use a calibrated thermometer, and do not cut the hold times short. Anova warns: never chill and reheat food that was cooked or held below 130°F / 54.4°C.

Quick workflow

  1. Preheat the bath to the bath temperature for the texture you want from the Serious Eats chart, and let it reach temperature before adding the turkey. For their favorite, that is 145°F / 63°C.
  2. Season, bag (vacuum or water-displacement), and fully submerge. Serious Eats and Anova both say wet brining is unnecessary for sous vide turkey. For even cooking they remove the two breast halves and tie them into a cylinder of even thickness, ties at 1-inch intervals.
  3. Probe the thickest part and start the hold clock only after the core reaches the chart temperature — the same idea as Sous vide turkey breast: probe first, timer second. Serious Eats: come-up can take an hour or more; check after the first hour and every 15 minutes, or use a leave-in probe. Then hold for the chart’s post-core time, or cook for the approximate total time (2½ hours at 145°F). From frozen, Anova says to allow an extra hour to fully thaw before you begin timing for doneness.
  4. Finish for crust, not for doneness: pat very dry. Anova’s skillet finish is medium-high heat, skin side down, until the skin is deep brown and very crisp, about 2 minutes — the same idea as Crispy sous vide turkey: dry first, crisp second. Serious Eats and Anova also roast the skin separately at 400°F / 200°C for about 30 to 45 minutes, sandwiched between parchment and two sheet pans. For a smoke finish, see Sous vide turkey: bath first, smoke second.
  5. Verify with an instant-read thermometer if you are aiming for the USDA consumer minimum or a Baldwin Table 4.1 / FSIS Table 4 hold.

Make-ahead and leftovers

For a make-ahead bath, chill the sealed bag in an ice bath, then refrigerate — the same idea as Make-ahead sous vide turkey: bath first, chill second. After carving, cool promptly in smaller pieces (Leftover turkey: carve first, cool second) and store wrapped, following the FSIS leftover rules above (Leftover turkey storage: wrap first, store second). Serious Eats’ recipe note says the cooked turkey can be ice-bath chilled and refrigerated for up to a week, then reheated in a 130°F / 55°C bath for about an hour. That week is longer than the FSIS leftover window of 3 to 4 days in the fridge, and FSIS leftover reheating is 165°F, not 130°F. Use the FSIS rules for storage time and leftover reheating. Anova also warns never to chill and reheat food cooked or held below 130°F / 54.4°C.
